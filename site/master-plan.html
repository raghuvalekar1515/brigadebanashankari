How to read the Brigade Banashankari master plan

A master plan is the bird's-eye drawing of the whole site. When the detailed Brigade Banashankari plan is released, read it in this order:

  • Find the north arrow and the legend first, so you know which way the buildings face and what each colour means.
  • Find the entrance and trace the drive to each tower and to the basement ramps.
  • Locate the two towers and see what lies between them.
  • Find the clubhouse building and how far it is from each tower.
  • Follow the landscape: the gardens, the shaded paths and the gathering spaces.
  • Remember that a master plan is indicative and can change; the agreement for sale is what binds.

Brigade Banashankari: two towers on about 9 acres

Placing 600 homes in two towers is the plan's central decision. Each tower has four basement levels, a ground floor and 40 floors above. Building tall keeps the footprint of the homes small, which is how more than half the land stays open.

For a buyer, the tower matters as much as the floor. Which tower faces which direction, and what each looks out on, will be clear once the detailed plan is out, so keep your choice of tower open until then.

The clubhouse in the Brigade Banashankari master plan

The clubhouse is planned as a building of its own, with a ground floor and five floors above. On the site plan, that gives the club its own address within the community, separate from the entrances of the residential towers.

When the plan is released, check the walk from your tower to the clubhouse, and whether that path runs through the gardens. With 600 homes sharing one clubhouse, also look at where its entrance faces and how close it sits to the gathering spaces in the landscape.

The Brigade Banashankari master plan and your choice of home

The master plan is also the key to choosing a unit. The 600 homes are split between two towers: 400 of 3 BHK, 100 of 4 BHK and 50 each of 1 BHK and 2 BHK. How each type is spread across the towers and floors will show on the floor plates at launch.

If you want one of the scarcer 1 BHK or 2 BHK homes, find out early which tower holds them. If you want a 4 BHK, ask which floors they sit on. For a 3 BHK, the wide choice means you can let the master plan, the outlook and the distance to the clubhouse decide between units.

How the Brigade Banashankari site is shared out

Read the site in layers. Below ground, each tower has four basement levels. At ground level, about 4.8 acres, more than half of the 9 acres 1 gunta, is landscape: gardens, shaded paths and outdoor spaces for gatherings. Above it rise the two residential towers and the clubhouse building.

The plan provides 1,000 parking spaces. Where they sit, and how much of the surface stays free of cars, will be clear on the detailed plan, and it is one of the first things to look for.

What the Brigade Banashankari master plan will add at launch

  • The entrance, the internal drive and the ramps to the basements.
  • The layout of the landscape and where the gathering spaces sit.

Questions to ask about the Brigade Banashankari master plan

  • Which tower has the home type I want, and on which floors?
  • What does my tower face, and what lies between it and the other tower?
  • How far is my tower from the clubhouse and from the gathering spaces?
  • Where do cars enter, and how does the basement connect to my tower's lifts?
